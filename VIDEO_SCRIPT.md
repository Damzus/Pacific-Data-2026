# Video script — POSTER: "The island stays. The village goes."

Simple and non-technical, in the style of last year's winner. About 1:50.
Recording page: http://localhost:8765/record.html?src=site/poster.html (poster + round face circle).
Phone prompter: http://<mac-ip>:8765/prompter.html

---

### 1 · Who I Am (look at the camera)

*Poster at the TOP (title showing). Circle big (click it, press +).*

> Bula vinaka. My name is Saurab Nand, and I'm representing Fiji.
> My poster is called: The island stays. The village goes.
> This project is my contribution to telling the true story of climate change in the Pacific.

### 2 · The Idea (look at the camera)

*Stay on the title.*

> People often say the Pacific is sinking.
> I looked at twenty-five years of satellite images of our coastlines, across nine Pacific islands.
> And what the data shows is clear: that one sentence is wrong.

### 3 · Four Numbers (look at the screen)

*Circle small (press −). Scroll a little to the ROW OF FOUR NUMBERS. Point at each one.*

> At the top are four numbers.
> In Solomon Islands, five islands are already gone.
> But Funafuti in Tuvalu, often called a drowning nation, is gaining land.
> And Tarawa in Kiribati is losing it.

### 4 · Nine Islands (look at the screen)

*Scroll to "Nine islands. Nine different answers." Point at the maps.*

> Here are the nine islands.
> Every dot is a satellite measurement of the coast.
> Red is losing land. Blue is gaining land.
> Nine islands. Nine different answers.

### 5 · Fiji (look at the screen)

*Point at the second map: NATEWA BAY (Fiji).*

> This one is Fiji. Most of this coast is red.

### 6 · Two Charts (look at the screen)

*Scroll to the TWO GREY AND COLOURED CHARTS. Point left, then right.*

> On the left is how fast the sea is rising: about four to five millimetres every year, almost the same everywhere.
> On the right is what happened to the coast.
> If the sea decided everything, both sides would look the same. They don't.

### 7 · The People (look at the screen)

*Scroll to "Land is not the same measurement as home." Point at the MIDDLE box (Carteret).*

> And this is the most important part. Land is not the same as home.
> In Papua New Guinea, the island is growing.
> But about seventeen hundred people are being moved away, because the water turned salty and the gardens stopped growing.

### 8 · Fiji Village (look at the screen)

*Point at the RIGHT box (Fiji).*

> And here in Fiji, in 2014, a village moved two kilometres inland.
> It was the first village in Fiji to move because of climate change.

### 9 · My Message (look at the camera)

*Scroll to the BLACK BOX at the bottom. Circle big (click it, press +).*

> So the message I wish to share is this.
> The sea is rising under all of us. But what it costs is decided island by island.
> So before we plan, we should ask: which island? And who lives there?

### 10 · Thank You (look at the camera)

*Look at the camera and smile.*

> On that note, I'd like to thank the Pacific Dataviz Challenge team for this opportunity.
> Let's keep protecting every island in our Blue Pacific.
> Vinaka vakalevu.

*Smile and hold for two seconds, then stop recording.*

# How to make the video (Mac, free tools)

You already have **iMovie**. The easiest setup that needs nothing new installed is
**QuickTime + iMovie**. If you'd rather do it in a single take with no editing, use **Loom**.

## Option A: Loom (easiest, about 30 minutes)

1. Sign up free at loom.com and install the desktop app. The free plan allows 5-minute videos.
2. Choose **Screen + Camera**. Your face appears as a round bubble you can drag to a corner.
3. Open the site, hit record, and follow the script. To get the full-screen face shots
   for the opening and closing, click the bubble to make it bigger.
4. Trim the start and end in Loom, then click **Download** to get an MP4.

## Option B: QuickTime + iMovie (more control, about 1–2 hours)

**1. Record**
- Open **QuickTime Player**, go to **File → New Movie Recording**, and pick your camera.
  This window is your face bubble. Make it small, drag it to the bottom-right corner,
  and turn on **View → Float on Top**.
- Press **Cmd + Shift + 5**, choose **Record Entire Screen**, and under **Options**
  pick your microphone. Record the demo with your face floating in the corner.
- Separately, record the opening and closing on the webcam alone, full screen
  (QuickTime → New Movie Recording → red button).

**2. Edit in iMovie**
- Create a new Movie and drag in the three clips: opening, demo, closing.
- Trim out the pauses and mistakes. Cut wherever you want, because nobody expects one take.
- Add a title card at the start: *"The island stays. The village goes." — Saurab Nand, Fiji.*
- Optional: under the demo, add soft background music from iMovie's **Audio → Soundtracks**,
  turned down to about 10% so your voice stays clear.
- Export with **File → Share → File** at 1080p.

**3. Captions (recommended, since many people watch without sound)**
- **CapCut** (free, capcut.com) auto-generates captions. Import the iMovie export,
  choose **Text → Auto captions**, fix any misheard words (Vunidogoloa, Natewa), and export.

## Recording checklist

- **Light:** face a window. Daylight on your face, never behind you.
- **Sound:** a quiet room. Earphones with a mic sound better than the laptop mic.
- **Background:** something Fijian helps the human touch: a sulu or bula shirt, a masi
  (tapa) cloth, or a photo of the coast behind you. Keep it simple.
- **Camera:** at eye level. Prop the laptop up on books and look at the lens, not the screen.
- **Site:** run `python -m http.server 8765` and open `localhost:8765/site/`, or use the live
  URL. Use the **light theme**, zoom the browser to 110–125% so text is readable on video,
  and close other tabs and notifications (turn on Do Not Disturb).
- **Practise:** read the script aloud twice with a timer. If it runs past 2:00,
  cut words, don't speed up.
- **Delivery:** you don't need to memorise it. Put the script in a small window next to
  the camera. Talk as if you're explaining it to a relative back home.
